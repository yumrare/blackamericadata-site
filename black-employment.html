Black Employment

The Black Unemployment Rate: The Two-to-One Pattern

Black unemployment runs at roughly twice the White rate — 5.5 percent against 3.3 percent in annual averages — a ratio stable across five decades of federal data. How it is measured and why it persists.

Black Median Weekly Earnings: The 80-Cent Ratio

Black full-time workers earn a median of about $900 a week — roughly 80 cents for every dollar White workers earn. The BLS earnings data by group, gender, and occupation.

Black Labor Force Participation: Working at National Rates

Black labor force participation runs at roughly the national rate — and Black women participate at higher rates than White women. The BLS participation data.

Black Workers by Occupation: Where Black America Works

About 36 percent of Black workers hold management or professional jobs; about one in five works in service occupations. The BLS occupational distribution and its history.

Black Workers in the Public Sector: Government as Employer

About one in five Black workers works for a government — federal, state, or local — a share far above the national figure, with consequences across the employment data.
