Black Employment

The Black Unemployment Rate: The Two-to-One Pattern

Black unemployment runs at roughly twice the White rate — 5.5 percent against 3.3 percent in annual averages — a ratio stable across five decades of federal data. How it is measured and why it persists.

Black Median Weekly Earnings: The 80-Cent Ratio

Black full-time workers earn a median of about $900 a week — roughly 80 cents for every dollar White workers earn. The BLS earnings data by group, gender, and occupation.

Black Labor Force Participation: Working at National Rates

Black labor force participation runs at roughly the national rate — and Black women participate at higher rates than White women. The BLS participation data.

Black Workers by Occupation: Where Black America Works

About 36 percent of Black workers hold management or professional jobs; about one in five works in service occupations. The BLS occupational distribution and its history.

Black Workers in the Public Sector: Government as Employer

About one in five Black workers works for a government — federal, state, or local — a share far above the national figure, with consequences across the employment data.

Black Women in the Workforce: Participation, Pay, and Occupations

Black women work at the highest participation rates of any major group of women — about 62 percent — across every level of the economy. The BLS data.

Black Youth Employment: The First Rung of the Ladder

Black teenagers and young adults face unemployment rates roughly double the national youth figures — and the research shows the first job shapes the next decade. The BLS data.

Black Workers in Health Care: The Industry's Backbone

About one in seven Black workers works in health care — three million people, overwhelmingly women — spanning home health aides to physicians. The workforce data.

Black Workers in Technology: Eight Percent of the Defining Industry

Black workers hold about 8 percent of U.S. technology jobs — and a smaller share of the technical roles at the largest firms. The workforce data.

Black Americans in the Military: Service and the Veteran Workforce

Black Americans form about 17 percent of the active-duty force — and Black women the most overrepresented group in uniform. The Defense Department data.

Black Workers in Transportation and Logistics: The Freight Economy's Workforce

Black workers form about a fifth of America's transportation and warehousing workforce — the industry that moves everything the economy sells. The BLS data.

Black Workers in Manufacturing: The Industry That Built the Black Middle Class

Black workers form about a tenth of the manufacturing workforce — an industry whose union decades built the Black middle class and whose contraction reshaped it. The BLS data.

Black Workers in the Gig Economy: Platform Work in the Data

Black workers participate in platform and gig work at rates above the national average — in an arrangement whose flexibility and insecurity the surveys measure together.

Black Workers in Finance and Insurance: The Industry's Thin Upper Floors

Black workers form about a tenth of the finance and insurance workforce — concentrated in its operations floors and thinnest in its revenue roles. The BLS and industry data.

Black Workers in Retail: The Largest Sales Floor in the Data

Retail trade employs roughly a million and a half Black workers — in the economy's most accessible jobs and its most contested scheduling and wage data.

Black Workers in Construction and the Building Trades: The Apprenticeship Gate

Black workers form about six percent of the construction workforce — half their share of employment — in an industry built on apprenticeship gates. The BLS and trades data.

Black Workers in Media and Entertainment: The Visibility Paradox

Black culture drives American entertainment while Black workers hold a small share of its decision jobs. The Hollywood, newsroom, and music data.

Black Union Membership: The Highest Rate in the Labor Force

Black workers are the most unionized group in America — about 11 percent are union members — and the union wage premium is largest for them. The BLS data.

Black Workers in Agriculture and Food Processing: From the Fields to the Plants

Fewer than two percent of Black workers remain in agriculture — but the meatpacking plants and food factories employ hundreds of thousands. The BLS and USDA data.

Black Postal Workers: The Mail's Middle-Class Machine

About a quarter of the U.S. Postal Service workforce is Black — the institution that built more Black middle-class households than any single employer in America.

Black Domestic and Care Workers: The Occupation That Raised America

Domestic work was Black women's defining occupation for a century. Today the care workforce — home aides, childcare — remains its heir. The NDWA and BLS data.

Black Men of Prime Working Age: The Participation File

About one in ten prime-age Black men is out of the labor force entirely — the employment data's most examined margin. The BLS and research data.

Black Workers in Hospitality and Food Service: The Welcoming Economy's Workforce

About 1.7 million Black workers staff America's hotels, restaurants, and bars — at the service economy's wage floor and its most public face. The BLS data.

Black Workers in Professional and Business Services: The Office Economy's Middle

Professional and business services employ about two million Black workers — from law-firm staff to temp agencies. The BLS industry data.

Black Self-Employment: The Solo Economy and the Second Shift

About one in twenty Black workers is self-employed — the lowest rate among major groups — while side businesses run at far higher rates. The CPS and survey data.

Black Firefighters: The Firehouse Ladder and Its Rungs

Black firefighters make up roughly 8–9% of America's career fire service — a share built city by city against the consent-decree era's open fights. The labor data.

Black Social Workers and Counselors: The Caring Profession's Own Workforce

Black practitioners hold roughly a fifth of America's social work jobs — among the highest representation of any profession — at pay that trails the care they carry. The workforce data.

Black Pilots and Aviation Workers: The Smallest Cockpit in the Workforce Data

Fewer than 4% of America's airline pilots are Black, and Black women pilots number in the low hundreds. The FAA and workforce data behind aviation's steepest pipeline.

Black Lawyers and Judges: Five Percent of the Bar, Bench by Bench

Black attorneys are about 5% of America's lawyers — a share nearly unchanged in a decade of ABA counts — while the federal bench has reached its highest Black share in history. The legal profession's data.

Black Veterinarians: Two Percent of the Doctors of Animals

Black veterinarians are about 2–3% of the profession — among the lowest shares in any licensed field — in a country where Black households own millions of pets. The AVMA data.

Black Engineers: Five Percent of the People Who Build the Systems

About 5% of America's engineers are Black — a share that has barely moved in two decades despite the STEM era's billions. The degree-to-workforce data.

Black Journalists: Seven Percent of the Newsrooms That Cover 100 Percent of the News

Black journalists hold roughly 6–7% of U.S. newsroom jobs — half the population share — while the Black press that trained generations runs on a fraction of its historic scale. The workforce data.

Black Coaches and Sports Executives: The Pipeline Everyone Can See

Black athletes are most of the NBA and two-thirds of the NFL — and a tenth of NFL head coaches. The TIDES report-card data on sports' most visible workforce gap.

Black Pharmacists: The Most Accessible Clinicians, Counted

Black pharmacists are roughly 6% of the profession — trained disproportionately at four HBCU schools — staffing the counters the health system leans on most. The workforce data.

Black Dentists: Four Percent of the Profession That Owns Its Practices

Black dentists are about 4% of America's dentists — and dentistry remains the health profession where independent ownership is the norm. The ADA workforce data.

Railroad Workers: From Pullman Porters to the Modern Rails

The porters built a union, a middle class, and a movement on the rails. Today's Black railroad workforce in the BLS and industry data — the legacy file.

Black Executives and the C-Suite: Eight Chairs at the Fortune 500 Table

Black CEOs of Fortune 500 companies have never numbered more than a handful at once — about 1-2% of the chairs for 13% of the workforce. The executive data.

Black Auto Workers: The Line That Built the Middle Class

Auto work made more Black industrial fortunes than any other line — and its union was the movement's treasury and battleground. The UAW-era data and today's EV transition file.

Black Nurses: The Backbone Profession's Own File

Nursing is America's largest health profession, and Black nurses — about 11% of RNs and a fifth of LPNs — carry its history from the segregated training schools to the charge desk. The workforce data.

Child Care Workers: The Workforce That Makes All Other Work Possible

Black women are roughly a fifth of America's child care workforce — paid near the bottom of the entire wage table to do the economy's most foundational job. The workforce data.

Sanitation Workers: The Most Dangerous Essential Job in the City

Refuse collection ranks among America's most fatal occupations, and its workforce is heavily Black — a file that runs from the Memphis strike to the modern injury tables.

Longshoremen: The Dockworkers Who Moved the World's Cargo by Hand

Black longshoremen were the muscle of the Atlantic and Gulf ports for a century — a union stronghold whose numbers the container remade. The port workforce data.
